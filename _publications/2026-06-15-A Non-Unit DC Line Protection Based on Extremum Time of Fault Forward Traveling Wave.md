Compared with the traditional derivative-based traveling wave (TW) protection, the non-unit TW protection based on fault TW waveform analysis can effectively enhance sensitivity when high-resistance faults occur. However, protection based on fault TW waveform analysis may operate incorrectly due to inaccurate characterization of the initial fault TW, as well as the influence of subsequent TWs under line-port faults and the fault resistance associated with external faults. To address this issue, a non-unit DC line protection based on the extremum time of forward TW is proposed. Specifically, the differences in initial fault TWs associated with different fault locations are fully considered, together with the influences of fault resistance and subsequent TWs on waveform characteristics. The distinctions among different fault locations are then captured through criteria based on the extremum time of the forward TW and the fault voltage amplitude. Therefore, the proposed protection can effectively detect internal line-port and remote faults. The detailed model of the modular multilevel converter-based high voltage direct current (MMC-HVDC) transmission system is built in Real Time Digital Simulator (RTDS), and the proposed protection algorithm is programmed in the DC protection device. Hardware-in-the-loop test and simulation results show that this protection method can effectively address the problem of protection refusal under line-port or remote fault, and can withstand 400Ω transition resistance and noise with a signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) of 30dB.
